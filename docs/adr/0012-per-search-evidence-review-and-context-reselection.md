# Review per-Search evidence and reselect Answer context

Date: 2026-11-25
Status: Accepted — target design; not yet implemented or verified.

## Context

Agentic RAG must recover evidence in bounded Searches, retain distinct source provenance, and revise an answer when useful evidence was left outside its generation context. Reviewing the whole accumulated pool on every Search can exceed a provider's input capacity. A final top-k selection can also exclude evidence even when the pool has sufficient coverage. The controller's assessments and reranker scores are fallible; neither proves that all relevant information in the notes was found.

This decision records the agreed future behavior, not the behavior of the current checkout. Partially superseded decisions and the current implementation remain documented in [ADR-0003](0003-line-anchored-mode-specific-evaluation.md), [ADR-0008](0008-two-phase-agentic-evidence-recovery.md), [ADR-0009](0009-remove-local-context-window-budget.md), and [ADR-0011](0011-evidence-gated-answer-generation.md).

## Decision

### Retrieval and evidence review

- Classic performs one Search. Agentic Search and Answer generation have independently configured total-attempt limits, each including the initial attempt. Controller reviews and validation retries do not count as Search or Answer generation attempts.
- Each Search uses the configured Vector and BM25 top-k and thresholds, RRF fusion top-k, and Cross-encoder reranking. At most `LLM_MAX_CHUNKS` of that Search's ranked results enter the current query's pool; `FUSION_TOP_K`, filtering, available results, and deduplication may yield fewer. Agentic may rewrite the Search query or adjust the Vector and query-relative BM25 thresholds to recover missing information.
- Keep a per-user-query pool of original chunks and provenance; deduplicate by source and chunk ID across Searches. A new query starts with an empty pool, including in REPL; conversation history may clarify intent but is not evidence. The pool is not persisted across queries or sessions.
- After each nonempty Search, Retrieval review examines that Search's chunks against the original question, together with a source-linked evidence summary and remaining gaps from prior reviews. It records information found, supporting chunk references, missing information, and its resulting cumulative `zero_evidence`, `partial_evidence`, or `sufficient_evidence` assessment. It may plan a subsequent Search for zero or partial coverage when attempts remain. A later Search can fill earlier gaps; being sufficient for a rewritten Search query alone does not prove the original question is answered.
- Evidence summaries are model-generated assessments, not source evidence or guarantees of correctness. Preserve original chunks and provenance for generation and auditing; do not claim that the reviewer reread the entire pool. A summary or combined assessment can miss information or be wrong.
- An empty initial Search in either workflow stops processing and returns a fixed search-limitation message. If a later Agentic Search returns no chunks, stop further Searches and retain the earlier pool: proceed with partial evidence if previously assessed as partial; if prior coverage is zero, return a fixed no-related-information message without generation. Exhausting the Search budget with partial coverage likewise proceeds; exhausting it with zero coverage returns that fixed message. The no-related-information message includes the original user question and describes the observed search, without claiming the corpus lacks an answer or that the question is necessarily ambiguous.
- Retrieval-review failure, including provider or invalid-output failure after bounded validation, leaves coverage unknown and is a workflow error; it must not be treated as zero evidence. Preserve the available trace and provide an understandable warning.

### Generation and Answer review

- Once Retrieval stops with partial or sufficient coverage, preserve the complete per-query pool. If its size exceeds `LLM_MAX_CHUNKS`, rerank against the original question and select its top `LLM_MAX_CHUNKS`; otherwise retain the pool order and skip this final rerank. Do not add a pre-generation coverage check of the selected context. A high-ranked selection can still omit needed evidence or contain no supporting evidence.
- Each Answer generation uses at most `LLM_MAX_CHUNKS` original chunks. Keep the exact ordered context associated with each candidate. A grounded answer may be full or partial; partial answers disclose missing information. A model may also be unable to answer at all, return empty output, or fail; entering generation does not guarantee a usable answer.
- Answer review assesses the candidate against the exact context used to generate it, including relevance, completeness, missing information, and unsupported claims. A supported full answer can be returned without a warning. A supported partial answer is returned with its missing-information explanation if no useful additional evidence is found or revision is unavailable.
- When the answer has a gap and another Answer attempt remains, Answer review may examine unused pool chunks for helpful evidence: rerank the unused candidates against the original question and missing information, then review batches of at most `LLM_MAX_CHUNKS` chunks until useful evidence is found or the candidates are exhausted. Record which chunks were actually examined; an unexamined chunk is not evidence of absence. This candidate inspection is part of Answer review, not a separate workflow stage.
- Choose a revised generation context from the prior and newly selected original chunks, at most `LLM_MAX_CHUNKS`, and generate again with focused revision feedback. Same-context revisions remain possible when useful. Each new generation consumes an Answer attempt; do not reopen Retrieval. Reassess every candidate against its own exact generation context. If the answer needs more chunks than fit in one context, return a supported partial answer with gaps when a grounded full answer cannot be achieved; no multi-call answer synthesis is decided here.
- Do not inspect further pool candidates after the last Answer attempt: return the last reviewed result according to its status. If the last candidate is known to contain unsupported claims and attempts are exhausted, show it as an unverified candidate with the reviewer's reasons. If Answer review cannot finish, show the latest available candidate with a clear warning that review was not completed, without inventing a reason. If no usable candidate was generated, return a generation error. If a later revision generation fails, show the last available candidate with a warning that revision failed; do not claim it passed review unless established by its own review.
- If the inspected evidence supports no factual answer, return an explicit insufficient-evidence message rather than require an invented full or partial answer. An empty output or provider failure is a generation error, not evidence of absence.

### Failure reporting and evaluation

- Report Retrieval review, Answer generation, and Answer review failures separately, including the failed step, Search or Answer attempt, candidate-review batch where applicable, and available sanitized failure category or exception type. Distinguish provider failure, invalid controller output, empty generation, and judge failure where known. User-facing warnings should explain what failed without exposing credentials or guessing an unknown cause.
- Report per-query workflow and Answer review status and overall counts, distinguishing supported full and partial answers, unverified or rejected candidates, deterministic outcomes, and failures. Preserve each Search and Answer attempt's evidence and context trace, including the context associated with a displayed earlier candidate after a later failure.
- Evaluation remains isolated from runtime notes and indexes. Compute final context evidence metrics from the exact ordered chunks used to generate the candidate actually shown, not the complete pool or a later failed attempt. Preserve earlier attempt contexts for audit.
- Apply each Ragas metric whenever its actual required inputs are sufficient, independently of workflow/review status. This includes displayed unverified candidates and deterministic no-related-information responses for prepared unanswerable cases when the metric's real inputs are available. Use the actual displayed response and, when applicable, its actual generation context; do not invent an answer or evidence to make a metric eligible. Record missing inputs as unavailable and judge failures as failed, with reasons. A system-error-only outcome with insufficient metric inputs is not a fabricated candidate. Ragas scores remain diagnostics, not proof of correctness.

## Trade-offs and boundaries

Per-Search evidence summaries reduce the number of raw chunks in an individual Retrieval-review call but can lose or misstate cross-Search evidence. Removing the selected-context gate reduces a pre-generation review call but permits an answer attempt with inadequate selected evidence. Reranking is relative and cannot guarantee that all required chunks appear in the top-k. Showing an unverified candidate preserves user-visible output but risks exposing unsupported claims; clear warnings and separate reporting are required.

`LLM_MAX_CHUNKS` bounds a count, not prompt tokens. Query, history, evidence summaries, chunk lengths, and instructions also consume context. Provider limits may still reject or truncate inputs, and the design does not assume that a model reads every submitted token. A review that fails must not be described as a completed zero-evidence assessment. Exact token budgeting and a strategy for summaries that exceed provider limits remain implementation questions; no local context-window budget is introduced by this decision.

The user-facing workflow documentation and tests continue to describe current behavior until implementation. Migration must reconcile this ADR with the glossary, report schema and tests, and verify actual provider behavior separately from deterministic tests.
